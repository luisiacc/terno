# AI

AI is optional and off by default. It runs on a server on this Mac (Ollama, LM Studio, any OpenAI-compatible server) or in the cloud with your own key, and mail goes only to the provider you chose. Terno has no AI server of its own yet: hosted AI and Pro gating aren't built.

- **Providers (Settings → AI).** Local servers are discovered automatically (model, size, context, loaded state); *Test connection* checks latency and whether the model supports structured output, JSON mode or only text, and the app adapts. API keys are encrypted with the macOS Keychain and never reach the renderer (it only sees the last 4 characters).
- **Filters.** Described in plain language, then refined in a visual editor. The AI receives your description, account emails and names, category names and today's date, never message content.
- **Summaries of new mail ([plan 132](plans/132-ai-summaries.md)).** Turn on *Summarize new mail* in Settings → AI. A summary points out decisions, dates, actions and questions, and is kept on this Mac, so it isn't paid for twice. Only new mail that reaches the Inbox gets one automatically: not spam, not Promotions or Social, not your own, and at least 60 words. Turning it on sends nothing old. For older mail, *Summarize conversation* asks for one by hand. Summaries have their own provider and model, GPT Luna on OpenAI by default, and Terno asks before moving them to another provider.
- **Drafting ([plan 133](plans/133-ai-drafting.md)).** Press ⌘J in a draft, a conversation or the list, type what it should say or nothing at all, and press Enter. The draft arrives as one undo step, and nothing is ever sent for you. It writes in your voice, worked out from your sent mail on this Mac; *Let AI study my style* is the only part that sends samples, and it asks first. Instructions apply in order, later ones winning: general (Settings → AI → Drafting), the account's (Settings → Accounts), then what you ask for in the moment. Your style can be read, edited and cleared in Settings → AI → Writing style. It uses Claude Sonnet 5.5 when Anthropic is the provider, otherwise the provider and model you set. Settings says what each draft sends.
- **Categorization ([plan 134](plans/134-smart-categorization.md)).** Terno's own engine learns from how you file, needs no AI, and stays free. *Ask AI for help* in Settings → Categories adds a model for new mail the filters and the engine can't place, and sends it that mail with a few sender names and subjects from your categories. Your own provider only suggests; JEV, with a key in the same tab, files when it's very sure and suggests otherwise.
- **Ask your mail ([plan 141](plans/141-ask-your-mail.md)).** Press ⇧⌘K, or type a question in the palette: "What's the Airbnb door code?", "When does my lease renew?". A cheap model turns it into searches, and sees no mail on a first question. They run on this Mac, over all your mail except Spam and Trash. Claude Sonnet 5.5, or your configured model, then answers from the text of up to 30 matching messages, with each claim linked to its source. Links in an answer are never clickable, and a number or date that no source holds is pointed out. When this Mac doesn't hold the answer, *Search older mail* asks your own mail servers, only on that click, and keeps what it finds as normal mail. The first question to a provider or server says where it goes. *Previous AI questions* keeps your last 100 conversations, on this Mac only, and *Clear AI data* removes them.
- **Follow-ups ([plan 142](plans/142-promise-tracking.md)).** Off by default. *Keep track of what I promise and who owes me a reply* in Settings → AI has a small model read each message you send, and the one it answers. A promise comes back on the morning of its date, or after 3 days with none, and someone who hasn't replied comes back after the days you choose. Due ones sit on top of the Inbox until you open them; your own promises notify like reminders, and a nudge doesn't. A promise is only ever your own words, and a later message closes it only by quoting you. Turning it on offers one check of the last 30 days, with the count and the cost, and sends nothing old without a yes. Follow-ups under More lists them all.
- **Actions in plain English:** tell Ask what to do ("Archive shipping notices older than a month, except Amazon"). The model writes a rule, Terno finds the mail here, and the list shows the exact rows before anything runs. For meanings like "recruiters" it also checks senders and subjects, never the text, and can only remove rows. One Undo reverses all of it, and a sentence never forwards anything.
- **Replies ready:** off by default. When you open a conversation waiting for your answer and stay on it, Terno writes a reply in your voice below the last message. It isn't a draft until you use it, and it never sends itself. Nothing goes for mail you don't open.
- **Today:** with an AI provider set up, a row at the top of the sidebar lists what needs you: promises due, nudges, people waiting in conversations you're part of and reminders, all from what's on this Mac. The newsletter digest is its own opt-in: once a day, at a time you set, it sends the opening of up to 40 unread newsletters to your provider for a line on each, with an Archive all that has one Undo.
- **What it can spend ([plan 131](plans/131-ai-tasks-base.md)).** Settings → AI shows this month's use and sets a monthly limit for automatic AI. Automatic work also has daily ceilings. A request is counted before it leaves, an unknown price counts high, and what you ask for yourself still runs past the limits.
- **Your Terno account.** The first verified mailbox becomes the owner of this install (shown in Settings → Accounts). Removing that mailbox keeps it; changing it requires verifying the new mailbox again.
